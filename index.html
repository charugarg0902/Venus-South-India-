→
Kerala is an interesting reversal from the Veet picture
Kerala — Veet's strongest state — is comparatively weaker for Gillette Venus (Net +28.3%). Kerala has a deeply entrenched waxing and salon culture where threading and wax are seen as superior. The razor proposition has not displaced that preference here despite high digital literacy.
→
"Hair grows back thicker" myth is the primary cultural barrier
The single most repeated cultural friction point is the belief that shaving makes hair grow back coarser and darker — appearing across Tamil Nadu, Telangana and Kerala reviews. Unlike Veet, Gillette Venus has not run a myth-busting campaign to address this in India, leaving the belief unchallenged.
→
Karnataka is the strongest cultural fit
Bengaluru's urban, working-professional demographic is most receptive to the convenience-led razor proposition. Net +42.9% and lowest negative % (12.2%) make it the clearest beachhead market for Gillette Venus in South India.

!
Skin darkening is a stronger signal for Venus than for Veet in South India
Post-shave underarm and leg darkening is a more active negative theme for Gillette Venus than for Veet. The mechanism is different — friction from the blade vs chemical reaction from cream — but South Indian consumers associate both with darkening. The difference is that Veet has begun a myth-busting narrative; Gillette Venus has not.
!
Telangana carries the highest skin darkening concern volume
Telangana's 32% negative rate — the highest of any state — is disproportionately driven by skin darkening and razor burn keywords. Combined with the "hair grows thicker" myth, this creates a compounded perception problem that is pushing consumers back to salon waxing in this market.
→
"No darkening" is also a top positive keyword — the concern is address-able
Reviews that explicitly say "no darkening on my underarms" and "no razor burn" are among the most helpful-voted reviews in the dataset. Consumers are actively looking for this reassurance — and when the product delivers, they say so loudly. A targeted South India communication around the post-shave darkening myth could convert a significant portion of neutrals and fence-sitters.
